On Android, the bottom toolbar matches iOS 27's in more cases (simulator, side by side):

- Inside `NativeTabs`, a prominent button and a selected button's fill take the tab bar's tint, as iOS's toolbar does, where they were always the system blue. The navigation bar's keep the system blue, as iOS's do.
- Without a flexible spacer, the toolbar's capsules sit together in its middle, where they were packed to the left.
- `Stack.Toolbar.Spacer` with `sharesBackground` keeps the buttons either side of it on one capsule, its width added between them, where it always split them apart. Switched on or off, the icon after it melts into the capsule or splits off it, as the navigation bar's buttons do.
- A button's `separateBackground` no longer splits it off its neighbours' capsule in the bottom toolbar, which iOS ignores there, and `hidesSharedBackground` leaves it bare, with no glass, its neighbours' capsule split around it; with a bare button on it, the toolbar blurs and washes what scrolls under it, from 11pt above its capsules down, with a hairline along its top, as iOS's does. A lone icon's capsule beside a fixed space stays round. A bare item swapped in or out splits its capsule apart or melts it back together on the same split, fading in once the split has opened its place, and a button swapped into a capsule opens into it rather than popping in.
- `Stack.Toolbar`'s `tintColor` no longer colours its plain buttons, which iOS's doesn't either; a button's own `tintColor` still does.
- `Stack.Toolbar`'s `backgroundColor` fills its capsules in the solid fallback. Liquid glass ignores it, as iOS has no background colour for it.
- A `Stack.Toolbar.SearchBarSlot` with `separateBackground` or `hidesSharedBackground` is a magnifier in a circle of its own, as iOS 27 draws it, and the field grows out of it while the keyboard comes up, rising with it as it widens, where it was a field on a capsule of its own.
- Search from the toolbar's slot rides the keyboard up again: the field, mounted ahead of time, stayed pinned where it rested and opened under the keyboard.
- The magnifier's circle, among the toolbar's items or alone at the bottom, opens and closes with one motion: the keyboard comes up as the field buds off it, and drawing back, the field empties at once and only its glass travels into the circle, swelling as it nears it; the circle takes the swell over and settles with a wobble, its magnifier fading in once it has landed. Out of it, the field starts at the circle's size and widens as it rises.
- Opening search from the toolbar's slot, the field takes over the whole capsule's glass on its first frame, its content staying where it showed while the capsule's other items blur away, where its own glass came up as a second pill inside the capsule. The close button buds off the field a little later and stays inside it until it breaks free, and closing, melts back into it faster, the field drawing back as a plain pill.
- Closing search from the toolbar's slot, the field rides the keyboard down and widens back into the toolbar's capsule as its other items fade back in, as iOS 27's does, where it faded out where it stood. The toolbar's items no longer flash sharp for a frame as search mode starts, and a held toolbar capsule's ends are no longer cut square.
- In the solid fallback, the bottom toolbar's edge behind a bare button is the page's colour, what scrolls under it fading into it over 48pt as under the navigation bar's solid edge, where it was a hard grey band with a hairline. Glass views take `fallbackFadeTop` to fade at their top.
- In search mode from the bottom, where the navigation bar fades away, what scrolls up under the status bar still blurs and washes, as under a bar with no title, as iOS 27 keeps it, where it ran under the clock crisp.
- TalkBack: a toolbar item with a menu says it shows one; bare items and status views on the toolbar are reachable; the search field's clear button is out of reach while the field is empty; in search mode the faded navigation bar and the toolbar's hidden items are out of reach; and the field is read before its close button, where the close button came first.
- Touch targets: all of the back button's circle takes a tap, where the third before its chevron didn't; the navigation bar's 44pt items and magnifier take touches out to Android's 48dp, still drawn at iOS's size; and a search field's clear button takes them out to 48dp around its 17pt circle, where it reached 33dp.
