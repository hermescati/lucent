On Android, the tab bar's bottom accessory is pressed as iOS 27's is (measured on the simulator): pressed, it moves as every other glass button does, on the same spring, leaning toward the finger and lit most under it, and wobbling back on release; it grows as much as iOS's does, above the bar or minimized in its row; dragged off, it pulls after the finger: past an end it builds at about a glass button's pace and then holds (its middle 4pt toward a finger 87pt past), and above or below it keeps stretching the further the finger goes (7% taller and narrower at 630pt above it), its content stretching with it. Minimized, a press no longer opens the bar: the bar's own gesture took touches on the accessory, swelling the bar's pill and opening the bar on release, and kept the accessory's buttons from being pressed. The accessory draws under the bar, as iOS's does, so a search circle or lens dragged up over it passes in front of it, the lens showing the accessory through its glass as iOS's does, and over the bar only once minimized into the bar's row. Its content stays inside the capsule as it opens and closes, where the full copy's buttons stuck out past the still-narrow capsule's end. Its buttons take taps across their whole height: the tab bar's glass, drawn past the bar to have room to stretch, took the touches on the accessory's lower part, because Android's glass views ignored `pointerEvents`; they honour it now. Dragged off an end, it eases into the pull rather than jumping on at the edge, and a finger far above the minimized accessory that wanders past an end turns it gradually, where it took the whole side pull at once.
