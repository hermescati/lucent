On Android, the stack's swipe back moves as iOS 26's does, from recordings of it: the screen below dims, about 10% black at the start and clearing as the swipe goes, its title and bar background with it; the top screen moves from the first touch instead of jumping the distance the drag took to start; and on release it settles on iOS's spring, starting at the finger's speed, in about 200ms. The bar's buttons hold still while the pages and their titles slide, as iOS's one bar does, rather than sliding off with the screen; once the swipe lets go onto the screen below, the buttons it doesn't have pop out as iOS's do, blurring, swelling and fading over about 220ms, and the screen below's pop in. The screen going back rounds its corners to the display's while it slides and casts a soft shadow on the screen below, as iOS 26's does; to round its background too, Lucent now draws a page's `contentStyle` background itself when it comes from the stack's `screenOptions`. A swipe that starts at either edge of the screen is left to Android's own back gesture: before, both took it, and the screen below could be left stuck partway, without its bar. `fullScreenGestureEnabled: false` now turns Lucent's swipe off for a screen on Android, leaving Android's back gesture at the edge, as it keeps iOS's swipe to the edge. Glass on the screen underneath no longer draws over the screen above it while both show.
