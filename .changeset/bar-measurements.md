On Android, the navigation bar and bottom toolbar match iOS 26 more closely, from new measurements on the iOS simulator. Capsules have Apple's continuous corners, which ease into their straight sides instead of ending in a semicircle; so do glass buttons and the tab bar. Capsules sit 12pt apart rather than 8, icons in a capsule sit 54pt apart, text buttons get 17pt either side of their label, and the back button's label sits further from its chevron. The titles follow finger drags recorded on iOS: the large title rides up into the bar's row, then the two cross-fade on a clock once it has scrolled its height (150ms out, 300ms in) rather than with the scroll. A scroll that stops partway through a large title carries on to whichever end is nearer, in `TabScrollView`, or in a scroll view whose animated ref goes to the new optional argument of `useHeaderScroll`. A pushed screen's bar items fade in where they stand instead of appearing at once. The scroll edge washes the content under it more and blurs it less, as iOS's does, so text there shows through dimly instead of smearing away. A one-pixel hairline, white or black at 10%, marks the edge's bottom while content is under it, as on iOS. Bar buttons are drawn in the bar's glass, as the tab bar is: its softer rim and shadow, in place of a glass view's thin outline, which looked dimmer than the tab bar's search button in light mode. The chevron alone in the back button's circle sits 1.5pt left of centre, as on iOS. A screen whose page re-renders as its header settles no longer loops until React gives up ("Maximum update depth exceeded").
